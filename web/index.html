Method. Fixed dynamical heating: the surface and troposphere are held fixed and the stratospheric temperatures are changed until each layer's longwave cooling returns to its value in the 1750 atmosphere, so solar and dynamical heating balance it again. Only the longwave is recomputed, absorption cross-sections are kept at their reference-temperature values, and the model has 12 stratospheric layers above 12 km. What to read into the numbers: carbon dioxide and nitrous oxide agree with AR6 to about 2 %; the model's methane forcing is about 14 % lower than AR6's. The cause was not isolated (the single idealised profile, the idealised methane vertical profile and band overlap with water vapour are candidates), so treat the methane number as the least certain. The tropospheric adjustment factors (CO₂ +5 %, CH₄ −14 %, N₂O +7 %) are AR6's, not computed here.

Method, limits and ideas

Data and method. H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O and O₃: HITRAN2020 absorption lines (Gordon et al. 2022) with Voigt line shapes. Water vapour also includes the MT_CKD 4.3 continuum (Mlawer et al. 2012; AER). Halocarbons: HITRAN infrared cross-sections for 17 molecules. Layer-by-layer radiative transfer through the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere (idealised water-vapour and ozone profiles; halocarbons uniformly mixed). Only derived spectra are shown.

All-sky estimate. Clouds are represented by one grey deck (cover 0.67) whose opacity is tuned so the model's longwave cloud effect equals the satellite value of 27.9 W m⁻² (CERES EBAF; Loeb et al. 2018). It is a global-mean estimate, not a cloud model, and clear-sky values are shown for comparison.

Limitations. One atmospheric profile, no line mixing, one gas varied at a time, instantaneous forcing at the top of the atmosphere without stratospheric adjustment (so it is smaller than AR6's effective forcing for CO₂; the worked example above adds the missing steps). Halocarbon spectra exist only over the wavenumber ranges measured in the laboratory; outside them the lines are left blank, not drawn as transparent.

Ideas to try

  • Choose CFC-12, HFC-134a or SF₆ and slide the column from 10¹⁶ to 10¹⁹ molecules cm⁻²: the bands sit inside the window, which is why a few parts per trillion matter.
  • Raise CO₂ over many orders of magnitude and watch the 15 µm band flatten while its wings keep growing.
  • In the outgoing spectrum compare the forcing of 1000× today's SF₆ with the straight-line (AR6) prediction: strong bands begin to saturate.
  • Switch between clear sky and the all-sky estimate: window absorbers lose about a third of their forcing to clouds.

How this works, and what it leaves out

The model. A three-layer energy balance model: a thin surface layer exchanging heat with a mid and a deep ocean. It is the temperature part of FaIR v2 (Leach et al. 2021), with the heat capacities and exchange rates set to the median of the AR6-constrained calibration (Smith et al. 2024). ECS is changed by altering the climate-feedback parameter only.

The forcing. From one FaIR run per scenario using RCMIP emissions, with the same median climate parameters. It is a single deterministic run, not the full uncertainty ensemble, so the grey and coloured lines have no uncertainty band. The AR6 assessed range is shown for 2081–2100 instead.

Check against AR6. The reference setting gives 1.36, 1.73, 2.59, 3.44 and 4.27 K for 2081–2100 in the five scenarios, inside the AR6 very likely ranges and 0.1 K below its best estimates. For 2011–2020 it gives 1.00 K, inside the AR6 assessed 0.95–1.20 K but about 0.1 K below the thermometer record (HadCRUT5 1.12 K). ECS is 3.0 K and TCR 1.7 K.

Limits. The sliders change how the climate responds, not the emissions: concentrations and forcing stay as in the FaIR run. There is no regional climate, no weather and no tipping points. Past 2025 these are scenarios, not predictions.

How the metric changes with the time horizon

Compare with

Lines are computed here; diamonds are the AR6 table values. A gas that is gone within years (methane) loses most of its GWP as the horizon lengthens; a gas that lasts millennia (SF₆, CF₄) keeps its potency. GWP adds up forcing over the whole horizon, while GTP asks only about the temperature at one moment, so it forgets short-lived gases faster.

Why gases differ: lifetime against radiative efficiency

Every gas in AR6 Table 7.SM.7 that has a lifetime and a radiative efficiency. Up and to the right means a stronger absorber that also stays longer, so a higher GWP. Per molecule, halocarbons absorb far more than CO₂ (see the Spectra tab for the reason: they sit in the atmospheric window).

Method and limits

Method. For a 1 kg pulse, the absolute GWP is the forcing added up over the horizon and the absolute GTP is the temperature at the horizon, from a two-timescale climate response. Dividing by the same quantity for CO₂ (Joos et al. 2013 carbon cycle) gives the potential. The implementation follows IPCC AR6 WG1 Section 7.6.1 and includes the climate–carbon feedback, the CH₄ and N₂O adjustments and the ozone terms.

Checked against AR6. CO₂'s absolute values match the AR6 table within 0.1 %. For the 136 halogenated gases with a lifetime of at least a year, all five metrics here fall within 1.6 % of AR6 Table 7.SM.7 (135 of them within 1 %). Methane reproduces 27.9 and nitrous oxide 273.

Limits. Methane uses the non-fossil value (27.9); AR6 gives a higher fossil-methane value (29.8) that includes the CO₂ from its oxidation. Gases living under a year, and two with no usable formula, are shown with AR6 values only. Metrics are conventions for comparing emissions, not predictions of warming.

Glossary and naming guide

How to use: decode a gas name in the first box, look up any of the 22 molecules used on the Spectra tab in the table, or search the terms below by word or topic.

Decode a halocarbon name

CFC-11, HCFC-22, HFC-134a and PFC-218 all follow one rule: add 90 to the number. The three digits you get are the number of carbon, hydrogen and fluorine atoms. Every other bond on the carbons holds chlorine.

Data and methods

  • Forcing: IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 7 (Forster et al. 2021; Smith et al.)
  • Warming: FaIR v2 (Leach et al. 2021); HadCRUT5; NOAA Mauna Loa
  • Lines: HITRAN2020 (Gordon et al. 2022)
  • Water-vapour continuum: MT_CKD 4.3 (Mlawer et al. 2012; AER)
  • Cloud effect: CERES EBAF (Loeb et al. 2018)

Good to know

  • Equilibrium warming is ERF ÷ λ with λ = 3.93 ÷ 3 = 1.31 W m⁻² K⁻¹ (ECS 3 K). It is not today's observed warming, which is lower because the ocean has not yet equilibrated.
  • Spectral forcings are instantaneous, at the top of the atmosphere.
  • An educational tool, not a forecast.
