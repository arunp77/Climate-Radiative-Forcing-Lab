ℹ About this slider

CO₂ bands are strongly saturated near the line centres; extra CO₂ mostly adds absorption in the band wings, so forcing grows roughly with the logarithm of concentration: each doubling adds a similar amount (about 3.9 W m⁻² effective).

ℹ About this slider

CH₄ is far less saturated, so forcing grows roughly as the square root of concentration. It overlaps with N₂O near 7.7–7.8 µm, which the expressions account for.

N₂O forcing also grows roughly as the square root of concentration. Its strong bands lie at the edges of the 8–12 µm window, and it persists for about 110 years.

Method, limits and ideas

Data and method. H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O and O₃: HITRAN2020 absorption lines (Gordon et al. 2022) with Voigt line shapes. Water vapour also includes the MT_CKD 4.3 continuum (Mlawer et al. 2012; AER). Halocarbons: HITRAN infrared cross-sections for 17 molecules. Layer-by-layer radiative transfer through the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere (idealised water-vapour and ozone profiles; halocarbons uniformly mixed). Only derived spectra are shown.

All-sky estimate. Clouds are represented by one grey deck (cover 0.67) whose opacity is tuned so the model's longwave cloud effect equals the satellite value of 27.9 W m⁻² (CERES EBAF; Loeb et al. 2018). It is a global-mean estimate, not a cloud model, and clear-sky values are shown for comparison.

Limitations. One atmospheric profile, no line mixing, one gas varied at a time, instantaneous forcing at the top of the atmosphere without stratospheric adjustment (so it is smaller than AR6's effective forcing for CO₂). Halocarbon spectra exist only over the wavenumber ranges measured in the laboratory; outside them the lines are left blank, not drawn as transparent.

Ideas to try

  • Choose CFC-12, HFC-134a or SF₆ and slide the column from 10¹⁶ to 10¹⁹ molecules cm⁻²: the bands sit inside the window, which is why a few parts per trillion matter.
  • Raise CO₂ over many orders of magnitude and watch the 15 µm band flatten while its wings keep growing.
  • In the outgoing spectrum compare the forcing of 1000× today's SF₆ with the straight-line (AR6) prediction: strong bands begin to saturate.
  • Switch between clear sky and the all-sky estimate: window absorbers lose about a third of their forcing to clouds.

Data and methods

  • Forcing: IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 7 (Forster et al. 2021; Smith et al.)
  • Lines: HITRAN2020 (Gordon et al. 2022)
  • Water-vapour continuum: MT_CKD 4.3 (Mlawer et al. 2012; AER)
  • Cloud effect: CERES EBAF (Loeb et al. 2018)

Good to know

  • Equilibrium warming is ERF ÷ λ with λ = 3.93 ÷ 3 = 1.31 W m⁻² K⁻¹ (ECS 3 K). It is not today's observed warming, which is lower because the ocean has not yet equilibrated.
  • Spectral forcings are instantaneous, at the top of the atmosphere.
  • An educational tool, not a forecast.
